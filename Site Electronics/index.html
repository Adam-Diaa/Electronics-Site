Milestone 1: The radio's tuner and detector

A coil and a variable capacitor pick one station out of the air; a diode, a resistor and a capacitor pull its envelope back out. This is the front end that every later milestone builds on.

How it works

The tank circuit is a coil of inductance L in parallel with a tuning capacitor C. It resonates at one frequency, and a signal at that frequency builds the largest voltage across it (test point 1, TP1).

f0 = 1 / (2π√(LC))     Q = f0 / (fhigh − flow)

The detector is a peak rectifier: a 1N60P diode charges a 3.3 nF capacitor to the signal's peaks while a 15 kΩ resistor lets it discharge slowly. For a steady sine, test point 3 (TP3) holds a DC level; for a station's amplitude-modulated signal, it follows the sound.

Coil

75 turns of 0.3 mm enamelled copper, close-wound on a 50 mm PVC pipe, about 25 mm long.

Tuning capacitor

20 to 280 pF, fixed to the pipe, connected across the coil's AM section.

Detector

1N60P with its band towards TP3, then 15 kΩ and 3.3 nF in parallel to ground. RC ≈ 50 µs.

Weak signals sit in the diode's dead zone, so TP3 should stay near zero until TP1's peak is large enough to switch the 1N60P on.

Engineering challenges

  • Winding the coil.75 turns side by side with no gaps or crossings, and enamel scraped from both ends until the copper shone, otherwise the connection is poor.
  • Source frequency.The sound card reaches only about 20 kHz, while the band starts near 560 kHz, so a function generator was needed to reach the tank's resonance.
  • Diode dead zone.TP3 does not move until TP1's peak is large enough; the six source settings were chosen to span that threshold.
  • Coupling.The test loop must sit a few centimetres from the coil on the same axis, or TP1 stays small.

What comes next

M1 gives us a tuned circuit and a detector with no gain. Later milestones add amplification before the detector, so weak stations rise above the diode's dead zone, and after it, so the sound can drive a speaker.
